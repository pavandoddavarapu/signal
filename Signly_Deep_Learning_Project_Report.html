1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Domain Overview and Background

Communication represents an innate human necessity that underpins social integration, educational attainment, vocational independence, and healthcare access. According to epidemiological data provided by the World Health Organization (WHO), over 430 million people worldwide experience disabling hearing impairment, with more than 70 million deaf individuals relying upon Sign Language as their primary native linguistic medium. Unlike spoken tongues that convey phonetic information via vocal tract acoustic waveforms, sign languages are visually and spatially complex linguistic systems composed of manual components (hand shapes, palm orientations, spatial positions, and kinetic movement trajectories) and non-manual markers (facial expressions, eye gaze, and torso shifts).

Despite its morphological completeness, sign language remains an insurmountable communicative chasm for the hearing majority who lack formal sign training. Consequently, deaf individuals routinely experience profound isolation, institutional exclusion, and dangerous miscommunication during emergency, clinical, and administrative interactions. Certified human interpreters offer a partial remedy; however, they are economically prohibitive, geographically scarce, and inadequate for spontaneous everyday conversations. This urgent societal demand has galvanized artificial intelligence researchers toward developing real-time automated Sign Language Recognition (SLR) systems.

1.2 Motivation and Relevance of Assistive Technologies

Historically, early technological attempts to digitize sign interpretation depended on wearable sensory gloves laden with flex resistors, tactile switches, and inertial measurement units (IMUs). While computationally straightforward, physical gloves impose severe ergonomic burdens, cause sensor fatigue, demand frequent recalibration, and alienate users in public spaces. On the other hand, non-contact optical vision systems eliminate physical encumbrances, unlocking natural interaction. Recent advances in deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs), mobile-first object detectors, and topological landmark estimation offer an unprecedented opportunity to implement high-precision, low-latency sign language translation using ubiquitously accessible, consumer-grade webcams.

1.3 Project Overview: Signly Pipeline

Signly is a comprehensive, production-oriented sign language recognition and speech synthesis ecosystem engineered to translate American Sign Language (ASL) fingerspelling and control gestures into written English and audible vocalizations in real time. The software combines a cascade of advanced deep learning models:

1. Hand Detection: A custom-trained Ultralytics YOLOv8 network localizes hand regions within noisy video feeds and generates cropped bounding boxes.
2. Topological Landmark Extraction: Google MediaPipe Hands isolates 21 three-dimensional skeletal coordinate joints (x, y, z) representing hand geometry.
3. Canonical Normalization: Mathematical centering to the wrist joint origin and scale-normalization relative to knuckle Euclidean distance yield an invariant 63-dimensional feature vector.
4. Deep Classification: A TensorFlow/Keras Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) classifies normalized joint vectors across 29 discrete classes (26 alphabetic letters and 3 operational control commands).
5. Temporal Stabilization: A sliding-window consensus filter coupled with a hysteresis release latch prevents frame flickering and accidental repeating letters.
6. Sentence Construction: A stateful composition engine tracks additions, spaces, backspaces, and clears to form grammatical sentences.
7. Neural Speech Synthesis: Integration with ElevenLabs neural voice APIs converts finalized sentences into expressive, naturalistic human speech.

1.4 Core Objectives

The foundational objectives governing this engineering effort include:

• To achieve 100% non-invasive, purely optical vision-based sign recognition requiring zero wearable hardware or specialized depth cameras.
• To deliver sub-35ms inference latency, sustaining steady 25+ frames-per-second performance on commodity laptops.
• To attain mathematical scale and translation invariance, ensuring consistent recognition accuracy regardless of user distance from the camera or hand dimensions.
• To establish an active text composition system with real-time editing gestures ('Space', 'Delete', 'Clear') rather than isolated letter classifiers.
• To deliver multimodal assistive output by coupling visual transcription with state-of-the-art neural speech synthesis.

2. PROFILE OF THE PROBLEM. RATIONALE/SCOPE OF THE STUDY (PROBLEM STATEMENT)

2.1 Problem Definition & Real-World Challenges

Translating American Sign Language through unconstrained computer vision presents severe algorithmic difficulties that cause basic computer vision prototypes to fail in real environments:

• Ambient Environmental Inhomogeneity: Uncontrolled illumination, shadows, and complex dynamic backgrounds severely disrupt raw-pixel image classification networks.
• Anthropometric Variability: Significant differences in skin tones, hand proportions, camera angles, and distance to lens induce wide distribution shifts in raw visual datasets.
• High-Frequency Temporal Jitter: Video streams capture transitory finger movements between intended signs. An unstabilized classifier outputs erratic strings of gibberish during hand repositioning.
• Lack of Interactive Composition: Academic models often terminate after outputting isolated characters, neglecting the punctuation, spacing, and backspacing needed to compose real sentences.

2.2 Rationale of the Study

To overcome these fundamental obstacles, this study proposes a decoupled pipeline architecture. Instead of feeding unsegmented video frames into an enormous end-to-end 3D-CNN that acts as an uninterpretable black box, Signly separates the problem into localized detection, topological feature extraction, geometric normalization, and lightweight neural classification. This modular approach strips away skin pigmentation and environmental background noise, focusing purely on spatial geometric landmarks. As a result, the model achieves high accuracy with low computational overhead.

2.3 Scope and Delimitations of the Study

• Alphabetical and Control Sign Scope: The system is designed to recognize the 26 static letters of the manual ASL alphabet (A through Z) along with three operational control gestures ('Space', 'Delete', 'Clear'), encompassing a total of 29 target classes.
• Continuous Signing Delimitation: Dynamic full-body ASL involving torso motion, facial grammar, and two-handed continuous phrases is excluded from the current scope.
• Hardware Boundary: Designed for standard single-lens monocular webcams (standard 720p/1080p RGB streams at 30 FPS).
• Connectivity Requirements: Offline local execution is maintained for all vision processing and text composition. Internet access is only required for the optional ElevenLabs neural text-to-speech API.

3.4 What's New in the System to be Developed

Signly introduces critical architectural and operational innovations over prior systems:

1. Two-Stage Vision Cascade: Decoupled YOLOv8 hand bounding box localization and MediaPipe 21-joint skeletal landmark extraction.
2. Geometric Invariant Normalization: Wrist-centered origin translation and knuckle-based Euclidean distance scaling guarantee invariance to hand size and camera distance.
3. Anti-Bouncing State Machine Stabilizer: A sliding-window consensus filter (5 frames) and release latch eliminate noisy duplicate triggers.
4. Real-Time Editing Gestures: Incorporates functional editing signs ('Space', 'Delete', 'Clear') directly into the sign vocabulary.
5. Neural Speech Synthesis: Integrates the ElevenLabs API for multi-voice spoken audio output.
6. WebRTC Browser Architecture: Implements client-side video streaming via Streamlit-WebRTC, ensuring deployment compatibility across local and cloud environments.

6. DESIGN

6.1 System Design and End-to-End Architecture

Signly uses a decoupled pipeline architecture. Each processing stage operates as an independent filter receiving structured data from its predecessor:

[WebRTC Video Frame] ──> [YOLOv8 Hand Detection] ──> [MediaPipe 21 Landmarks] ──> [Geometric Normalization (63-D)] ──> [Keras MLP Classifier] ──> [Stabilizer State Machine] ──> [Sentence Builder] ──> [ElevenLabs Speech Synthesis]

6.3 Detailed Design & Mathematical Formulations

Given 21 landmarks P = {p_i = (x_i, y_i, z_i)}, where i ranges from 0 to 20:

1. Translational Centering:
c_i = p_i - p_0, where p_0 is the wrist joint origin (c_0 = (0, 0, 0)).

2. Knuckle Euclidean Scale Normalization:
s = ||c_9||_2 = sqrt((x_9 - x_0)^2 + (y_9 - y_0)^2 + (z_9 - z_0)^2)
s_ref = max(s, 10^-6)
c_hat_i = c_i / s_ref

3. Flattening & Standardization:
x = flatten([c_hat_0, c_hat_1, ..., c_hat_20]) in R^63
z = (x - mu) / sigma, using precomputed training mean mu and scale sigma.
